# Charge-sensitive R32 model design

Planned next milestone, 2026-10-10. This is a scientific design for review, not implemented charge/leak functionality. Existing live R32 code remains the reviewed quasi-steady model. All hardware geometry/controller values not directly sourced are generic assumptions. The owner has now requested real charge and slow-leak effects; previous deferral is superseded for this design work. Publication, oil, mixtures and proprietary manufacturer calibration remain separate work.

## Decision

Use a **small conservative refrigerant control-volume network with persistent mass M and internal energy U**, coupled to the existing room, coil-metal and outdoor film states. Compressor, expansion valves and most momentum relations can remain algebraic; fluid inventory and energy cannot disappear between solves. Begin with R32 and a homogeneous-equilibrium/no-slip fluid approximation, an explicit finite accumulator/separator and generic bounded EEV control. This is one-dimensional lumped/finite-volume thermofluid modeling, not FEA or CFD.

A steady charge-balance solve with floating subcooling is useful for initialization and equilibrium comparisons. A sequence of independent steady snapshots is insufficient for continuous leaks, weather changes, room Off, reversal and defrost: solving a new charge distribution also changes stored refrigerant energy. A **conservative grouped-pressure DAE is a legitimate alternative** if it retains the regional mass/internal energy, derives inventory from its declared phase profiles, and includes the actual storage derivatives in the component balances. That change must be supplied by physical heat/work/enthalpy fluxes, not hidden as a configuration correction. A full acoustic momentum PDE is unnecessary for this purpose.

The disposable pilot first tests two equilibrium pressure-region reservoirs: four fluid states `(M_L,U_L,M_H,U_H)`, two finite metal temperatures, paired compressor/valve enthalpy fluxes and a HEM nozzle. This is substantially smaller than the 22-cell graph. It tests conservative regional dynamics and cost; its uniform-temperature reservoirs do **not** reproduce distributed condensation/evaporation zones or a multisplit. Production may group equal-pressure storage while retaining axial heat-transfer profiles and separate inactive-branch inventories. Choose the smallest reduction that passes spatial/temporal and reversal tests, rather than commit to 44 fluid states before measuring them.

The density/internal-energy flash and integration performance pilot is a prerequisite before committing to a large coupled implementation. Failure of that pilot is a real numerical/performance blocker, not a reason to add a charge multiplier to COP.

## Why the present closure cannot simply acquire a charge slider

Current `cycle.ts` calculates two shared operating pressures with compressor throughput and exchanger/return residuals, generic 3 K superheat and 1 K subcooling targets (with cooling/defrost complementary minimum-lift control), and no retained refrigerant mass. It derives branch flows from finite refrigerant-side conductance or the generic idealized EEV allocation. It cannot determine an absolute charge without coil/line/header/accumulator volumes and a phase-distribution model.

In a conventional steady model, add `M_inventory(p_L,p_H,outlet_state,geometry)=M_specified` and free a real degree of freedom, normally condenser outlet enthalpy/subcooling or accumulator liquid holdup; do not keep both exact SH and SC plus add charge as another equation without an extra unknown. The ORNL input guide explicitly distinguishes charge-calculated and charge-balanced modes, supplies compressor/accumulator geometry and offers several void-fraction closures. Those are evidence for inventory closure, not Daikin calibration. [ORNL HPDM input guide, charge/superheat and geometry sections](https://web.ornl.gov/~jacksonwl/purez/hpdata.html).

For dynamics, mass/density as a primary state avoids reconstructing conserved charge from independently integrated pressure/enthalpy errors. MERL demonstrates the mass-drift problem and density-state alternatives in finite-volume vapor-compression models; its simplified homogeneous model omits oil. Our specific M,U formulation below is a design choice based on conservation, not copied software or a claim that MERL validated this simulator. [Laughman and Qiao, MERL TR2015-109, §§2–3](https://www.merl.com/publications/docs/TR2015-109.pdf).

## Physical graph and geometry

Maintain fixed physical component identities through mode changes. Four-way valve connections change; compressor suction/discharge ports, coil volumes, field lines and the service leak location do not exchange inventories merely because their roles reverse.

| Physical storage | Minimum proposed representation | Data required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outdoor refrigerant coil | Initially 3 equal-geometry axial cells; refine to 6/12 for comparison | Actual/declared internal tube volume, circuit layout/flow area, volume-weighted refrigerant UA |
| Each indoor refrigerant coil | Initially 3 axial cells, same physical cells in both directions | Per-unit internal tube volume and UA distribution |
| Each small and gas field line | At least one retained volume per line; spatial refinement if thermal/pressure gradients require it | Real one-way length, inside diameter, elevation and wall/ambient heat assumptions |
| Outdoor/indoor gas headers and liquid/distributor header | Explicit small finite mixing volumes where paths meet | Declared cavity volumes; no duplicate counting of connecting tubes |
| Compressor shell/dead space | A declared suction/discharge cavity only where its thermodynamic connection is known | Unknown internal compressor structure; no casing-volume inference |
| Accumulator | Finite gravity-separating equilibrium cavity with vapor pickup and explicit limits | Volume, vapor pickup/maximum liquid fill, optional heat transfer |
| EEVs/reverser | Algebraic negligible-storage restrictions; include real cavities in adjacent declared volumes if material | Flow-area/pressure-loss assumptions and valve state |

Three coil cells are a starting resolution to test, not an accuracy claim. Total count scales with declared port count, not an arbitrary five-room limit. A prototype with N rooms and no additional line subdivision has approximately `3(N+1)+2N+3 headers+1 accumulator = 5N+7` fluid volumes before any separately justified compressor cavities: 12 for one room, 22 for three, 37 for six. There are two conserved fluid unknowns per volume plus algebraic flows and controller states. These counts are a budgeting proposal and may need reduction or refinement after the pilot.

Field volume is always `V=π D_inside² L/4` for each physical small/gas line. It must remain present when pressure loss/line heat is idealized. The current runtime zeroes `pipe.lengthM` for its idealized transport reference; the new model needs **separate physical geometry and transport approximation**, rather than reusing that zero as inventory geometry. A transport-mode toggle must not remove charge or stored energy.

For illustration only, the owner-recorded total one-way branch length 28 m with declared minimum-wall bores 4.75/7.92 mm would give small-line volume 0.4962 L and gas-line volume 1.3794 L. These are derived volumes for those assumed bores; lengths/thickness and actual installation remain unverified. Even idealized piping would hold refrigerant in these volumes. Manufacturer pipe-charge add-on rules are not equivalent to continuously filling every small line with liquid, and cannot identify individual coil volumes.

Unknown outdoor/indoor internal refrigerant volumes are the principal absolute-charge data gap. External casing dimensions, finned area, refrigerant UA or nominal capacity do not independently determine tube volume. Supply a generic geometry record with provenance and sensitivity bounds; do not silently choose a liquid fraction or hidden volume to force the owner unit's factory mass to match.

The primary-source agent's separate [charge source register](refrigerant-charge-source-register.md) records the manufacturer charge evidence, geometry gaps and inventory experiments. Source observations support sensitivity to void fraction and internal geometry. No retrieved primary source gives a complete internal-volume inventory for the owner's exact assembled N8/FTXA installation.

## Conserved states and EOS

For rigid physical cell j with V_j>0, retain:

`M_j [kg]`, `U_j [J]`, `ρ_j=M_j/V_j [kg/m³]`, `u_j=U_j/M_j [J/kg]`.

Recover `(p_j,T_j,h_j,s_j,x_j)` from the pinned R32 EOS at `(ρ_j,u_j)`, with `h_j=u_j+p_j/ρ_j`. State quality is a mass quality for pure R32; single-phase quality remains undefined. Equilibrium two-phase density and quality give

`1/ρ = (1−x)/ρ_l + x/ρ_v`,

`α = (x/ρ_v)/((1−x)/ρ_l+x/ρ_v)`,

`M_v=α ρ_v V`, `M_l=(1−α)ρ_l V`.

This initial closure assumes common thermodynamic equilibrium and equal phase velocities in transport. Stored mass quality, vapor volume fraction and transported vapor mass fraction then have a coherent relationship. A slip correlation cannot be inserted into stored density while leaving the same homogeneous stream enthalpy/quality unchanged. A later drift-flux or two-fluid closure needs separate stored/flow phase relationships, positivity, energy flux and experimental validation. HEM conservation is achievable; quantitative charge distribution accuracy is not established merely by conservation.

Public `PropsSI` already supports the required R32 D/U flash in a bounded smoke check. A new reusable-state implementation must preflight a **computed P or T**, not `D` or `U` which are now input values and may be echoed without flashing. The current PH/PT density preflight must not be blindly copied. Keep invalid trials inside CoolProp's public error boundary; do not revive unsafe embind exception handling or private ABI patches.

## Component balances and global ledgers

For a signed face flow f joining A→B, calculate one upstream enthalpy and one mass flux once. Positive flow uses A's outgoing state; negative uses B's. Insert the exact same mass/enthalpy flux with opposite signs in neighboring balances. Junctions mix by mass/enthalpy; regularized zero-flow rules must not inject arbitrary energy.

For each fixed cell:

`dM_j/dt = Σ ṁ_in − Σ ṁ_out − ṁ_leak,j + ṁ_charge,j`.

`dU_j/dt = Σ ṁ_in h_in − Σ ṁ_out h_out + Q_ref,j + W_ref,j − ṁ_leak,j h_leak,j + ṁ_charge,j h_charge,j`.

Internal transport is low-kinetic-energy/declared-height unless its momentum treatment includes retained kinetic/potential energy consistently. The Modelica fluid library distinguishes stored internal energy from transported enthalpy and documents the limits of neglecting kinetic energy and the need for explicit junction models outside them. [Modelica Fluid component balances and stream conventions](https://doc.modelica.org/Modelica%204.0.0/Resources/helpDymola/Modelica_Fluid_UsersGuide_ComponentDefinition.html).

The initial pilot uses zero elevation. A nonzero-height implementation must either retain `E_j=U_j+M_j g z_j` and transport `h+g z` at each face, or derive the equivalent internal-energy gravity terms. Do not simultaneously add those terms and reuse the old steady pipe `h−gΔz` update; that would double count gravitational conversion. Kinetic energy neglected by this reservoir reduction requires a bounded low-velocity inlet/outlet approximation; a valve throat's kinetic energy is dissipated downstream and its transported **total** enthalpy remains upstream h.

The algebraic compressor removes `ṁ_c h_S` from the suction-side storage and adds `ṁ_c h_D` to the discharge side:

`ṁ_c=ρ_S V_disp n η_vol`,

`h_D=h_S+(h(p_D,s_S)−h_S)/η_is`,

`P_ref=ṁ_c(h_D−h_S)`, `P_comp,electric=P_ref/η_drive`.

No compressor mass storage is implied by swept displacement. Real dead-space/shell inventory requires a separately specified cavity. Require dry admitted vapor, discharge and ratio limits. During reversal, a strict compressor hold until p_D≥p_S can leave a persistent thermosiphon ordering. The implemented generic low-lift branch instead uses commanded positive displacement with h_D=h_S when p_D<p_S: pressure potential dissipates, ideal shaft work is zero, and actual downstream EOS entropy must not decrease. Mechanical friction and no-load electrical consumption are excluded, so COP is unavailable for any interval containing this branch. This is an explicit constitutive approximation, not an OEM negative-lift map or controller. Its enthalpy balance follows the adiabatic zero-work control-volume/throttling equations; those equations do not establish an actual compressor's throughput. [MIT control-volume energy](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/SPRING/thermodynamics/notes/node18.html), [MIT throttling](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/SPRING/thermodynamics/notes/node16.html). Drive losses retain their explicit outdoor destination during normal compression.

Each coil cell receives `Q_ref,j=UA_ref,j(T_metal−T_ref,j)`. The corresponding metal receives `−Q_ref,j`; reuse the existing moist-air/fan/frost balances with that heat replacing the old steady `mΔh` coil term. `ΣUA_ref,j=UA_ref,total` for the declared geometry. Finite cells initially use a well-mixed closure and therefore change the spatial heat-transfer approximation: a 3→6→12 refinement and frozen-metal comparison against the current enthalpy-integral steady coil are required. Do not claim identical heat transfer simply because total UA was preserved, or silently retune UA to hide discretization error. Existing air-side dirt/frost still changes air transfer and film evolution; it must not directly multiply refrigerant mass or COP.

The whole-model stored energy becomes `E_total=ΣU_ref+E_rooms/fabric/metals+E_outdoor_metal/film` (plus any explicitly retained pipe/compressor wall or mechanical energies). Its boundary ledger includes electrical work, ambient/envelope/ventilation/moist-air exchange, drains and **leak/charging enthalpy**. Define independent refrigerant mass residual

`R_M=ΣM_ref−M_initial−M_added+M_leaked+M_recovered`.

Internal refrigerant flows cancel; compressor refrigerant work remains an energy source equal to its explicitly allocated electrical part. Existing thermal/water residuals remain separate. A constant internal-energy reference is harmless only when mass source/sink enthalpies use the same EOS reference. Never remove `ṁ u` instead of `ṁ h`, or subtract an extra latent heat already represented by EOS energy change.

## Accumulator, phase regimes and controls

Model the accumulator as finite storage, not as an operation that deletes liquid from suction. In a two-phase equilibrium cavity, a vapor pickup admits saturated vapor with local `h_v(p)`; a single-phase vapor cavity admits its actual bulk h. Retained mass/internal energy determines boiling, cooling and pool size. A flow can consume initial headspace vapor plus vapor demonstrably produced through the energy-conserving phase evolution; it cannot empty a vapor reservoir while claiming unlimited dry suction. Resolve headspace exhaustion/maximum liquid fill events and stop the compressor for carryover risk. No magic vaporization heat or unlogged liquid return is allowed. First-pilot accumulator heat can be declared adiabatic; adding ambient/casing heat later needs its own UA/energy destination. Oil return and refrigerant dissolved in oil are not modeled in the first R32 inventory milestone.

Replace exact SH/SC constraints with **bounded actuators and measured outputs**. A generic EEV opening a_i∈[0,1] controls actual flow from local upstream state and Δp; an anti-windup PI controller seeks evaporation outlet superheat. If SH exceeds target, its usual action opens the valve, provided pressure/phase authority exists. In cooling individual valves can use each evaporator outlet sensor. In heating the common outdoor evaporator outlet supplies a common target for outdoor branch EEVs, with declared branch allocation/control assumptions. Accumulator separation may yield dry saturated compressor suction while liquid is retained: evaporator-outlet superheat and compressor-inlet superheat are not necessarily identical sensor quantities.

**Concrete pilot EEV closure:** a generic adiabatic equilibrium nozzle with effective opening `A(a)` and declared discharge coefficient. At upstream `(h_u,s_u,p_u)`, evaluate `G(p)=ρ(p,s_u) sqrt(2[h_u−h(p,s_u)])` for `p_d≤p≤p_u`; `ṁ=C_d A(a) max G(p)`. The maximizer is the throat pressure; if interior, further downstream pressure reduction does not raise flux. Transport upstream h through the restriction, with throat velocity dissipated into the downstream volume. Reverse flow swaps the actual upstream state. The core uses a 13-point logarithmic pressure bracket and 32 golden-section refinements, retaining endpoints. Exact-input memoization changes no EOS inputs. The disposable pilot was checked against a 501-point reference; additional production-state breadth remains required. This assumes equilibrium flashing/no slip; short-tube metastability and actual valve geometry remain uncertainties, so it is a generic restriction rather than an R32 OEM coefficient. Initially allow subcritical liquid/two-phase/vapor upstream states only when all isentropic flashes are admissible; near-critical states, unstable domains or unavailable maxima reject. No ideal-gas choking formula is substituted for flashing R32.

Pilot constants are explicit, **not proposed OEM defaults**: `C_d=.65`, fully open area `2×10⁻⁷ m²`, displacement `8×10⁻⁶ m³/rev`, speed20rev/s, volumetric efficiency.8 and isentropic efficiency.7. Fixed opening isolates conservation/constitutive feasibility; production adds bounded controller dynamics after the passive loop is verified.

Accumulator vapor selection is an explicit exception to homogeneous stream transport: one gravity-separating cavity, homogeneous equilibrium bulk storage and a separate vapor outlet. Its outlet h/s/density are saturated-vapor values while two-phase, actual values while dry vapor. Resolve positive gas headspace and pickup exposure; the pilot uses a declared minimum vapor volume fraction.02 as a **generic geometry guard**, not a universal carryover threshold. Dynamic depletion can boil additional liquid only through the M/U balance. Time-step refinement/phase exhaustion tests must reject a step that crosses an unrepresented submergence event; inspecting only the final phase is insufficient for a production event integrator.

Subcooling is a charge-dependent condenser-output diagnostic, not a simultaneous exact 1 K invariant. For an outlet in the saturation region display SC=0 with actual mass quality; for superheated vapor mark liquid subcooling undefined and identify loss of liquid supply. Similarly, only vapor can have positive superheat; a wet state needs its actual quality, not an artificial negative temperature offset. Charge deficit may eventually saturate the valve, starve liquid supply and raise SH; excess charge may store liquid in the accumulator or flood available condenser volume and increase pressure. These effects depend on geometry/control; no universal symmetric penalty or monotonic COP slope is assumed.

All cells permit vapor/two-phase/liquid transitions through EOS M,U states rather than changing the count of physical volumes. Optional phase-zone diagnostics may sum phase occupied volumes, but must not redraw or renormalize mass. Near-critical states, full-liquid low-compressibility conditions, wet compressor admission, unsupported valve entrance regimes and bad flashes require explicit guards and transactional rejection.

## Leak boundary and charging semantics

A leak belongs to a **fixed physical node/port**. For example, the common field gas service connection has different pressure/thermodynamic states in heating and cooling; it cannot be permanently assigned to p_L. Port names are not phase guarantees. Evaluate the outgoing fluid state locally; a gas pickup on a separated vessel is a distinct boundary assumption from a homogeneous pipe breach.

The first scientifically complete leak feature can use a clearly labelled **prescribed slow mass loss** (g/day converted to kg/s) at a specified local port. This is an imposed boundary condition, analogous to a specified load, not a prediction from an unknown hole. It still produces real charge/pressure/heat/control changes through M,U. Remove `ΔM=ṁ_leak Δt` and `ΔU=ṁ_leak h_local Δt` using the same accepted quadrature and integration substeps. Local outward-pressure/phase/available-inventory conditions must be checked. If the pressure reaches ambient, air ingress is outside the pure-R32 model; report that boundary rather than continuing a fictitious outward leak or silently adding air.

A pressure-dependent mechanical leak can be an additional explicit generic restrictor model. Unknown hole area, path length, discharge coefficient and metastable flashing preclude an installed-appliance leak-rate claim. Gas choking and flashing two-phase discharge must be distinguished; a plain incompressible `sqrt(ρ Δp)` law across a large refrigerant pressure drop is insufficient. NIST restrictor experiments show dependence on upstream subcooling/pressure and geometry, with non-ideal choking; those R22/other-fluid correlations are not an R32 pinhole calibration. [NIST refrigerant restrictor experiments](https://www.nist.gov/publications/experimental-investigation-and-modeling-flow-rate-refrigerant-22-through-short-tube), [NIST single/two-phase short-tube study](https://www.nist.gov/publications/mass-flow-rate-correlation-refrigerants-and-refrigerant-mixtures-flowing-through-short).

An initial-charge setting applies to fluid initialization, not to an unlogged live rescaling of every cell. Live charge/recovery, if offered, is an explicit boundary operation with specified feed/recovery port, mass and enthalpy. A charge addition must carry feed h and permitted supply pressure; removal carries the local recovery h. Thermodynamic initialization/recovery that cannot satisfy the specified mass, volume and energy must reject. Reset may explicitly begin a new scenario ledger; a leak or mode change may not.

## Off, reversal, defrost and physical edits

Turning an indoor unit Off stops its forced fan and closes its declared EEV/control path. Its coil and field-line volumes remain in the circuit. A generic gas-side-connected closed-EEV branch can redistribute/condense refrigerant while not delivering forced air; actual N8 auxiliary-valve control is unknown. Do not delete inactive inventory, instantly fill it with guessed liquid or assume full isolation unless the corresponding physical valves are explicitly modeled.

All Off immediately stops compression according to the accepted user override, but passive fluid/metal/room/film heat and permitted leak continue. Minimum-off timing constrains subsequent compressor start. Unlike the old inventory-free model, persistent fluid states can supply local pressure/T during Off if the new model resolves them; the UI must distinguish these from running flow/supply-air readings.

Reversal keeps all physical M,U and metal/film states. Ramp finite valve connectivity with n=0, then restart only after pressure direction, vapor pickup and controller conditions are admissible. Algebraic momentum permits thermodynamic redistribution without resolving acoustic waves. Changing every coil instantaneously from p_H to p_L as a relabeling is forbidden. Dynamic local pressures will replace the assumption of exactly two spatially uniform pressures; compressor port pressures remain the high/low operating summary.

Defrost reverses the physical connections, uses finite indoor-fluid/metal/room energy sources, and sends heat through refrigerant cells into the conserved outdoor film/metal. No initial ice/mass/metal reset, charge redraw or melt-energy shortcut. Retained ice may prevent a successful restart at low charge; preserve storage and report an actual control/domain failure or timeout.

Adding/removing/replacing a room or changing field geometry now alters the refrigerant circuit, not only room thermal storage. It needs explicit physical inventory handling (closed isolated component recovery/removal, evacuated volume connection, or new-scenario initialization). A pipe-volume edit cannot create/remove fluid mass or thermodynamic energy merely to retain old pressure. Until that operation is specified and validated, require restart/recovery for fluid-geometry edits; ordinary setpoint/weather/cleanliness/Off edits preserve all fluid states. Existing thermal configuration impulses remain legitimate for externally added/removed room air/fabric, but are not a substitute for refrigerant conservation each physics step.

## Initialization, nominal charge and factual limits

Mass alone does not determine a thermodynamic initial state: require geometry and an energy/temperature condition. One reproducible cold initializer is equal bulk density `ρ=M_total/ΣV` across initially connected cells at a declared fluid temperature: compute `(p,u)` from `(T,ρ)`, assign `M_j=ρV_j`, `U_j=M_j u`, and validate EOS/domain/pickup. This is a homogeneous initial mixture, not a claim that gravity puts liquid evenly in every coil. A gravity-equilibrated alternative needs an explicit pool allocation and elevation/connectivity constraint. Supplying M and T determines initial U; supplying M and U instead requires solving T and must not additionally impose an incompatible temperature. Initialize metal/room temperatures independently. Isolated pockets require their own initial masses/energies, not a guessed share recomputed every start.

The disposable operating pilot instead supplies explicit low/high saturation temperatures and mass qualities: `V_L=1L`, `V_H=.5L`, `T_L=0°C`, `T_H=40°C`, `x_L=.7`, `x_H=.05 or .15`; EOS determines each M/U. Finite metals start10°C/30°C with10kJ/K capacity each and40W/K fluid conductance. The resulting generic charge is an output of this chosen initialization. Varying x_H compares different actual initial masses/energies, not identical-energy scenarios or factory-charge percentages. Production user-charge initialization needs the cold/steady constrained solve before exposure.

For a charge-balanced steady manifold initializer, free condenser outlet enthalpy instead of fixed SC; solve the two previous cycle closure equations plus `Σρ_j V_j=M_specified`, with phase-zone inventories evaluated from the same heat-transfer solution. If SH actuator saturation is allowed, its actual EEV/control equation replaces an exact SH target. A subsequent dynamic reduced DAE must additionally retain regional U/M: steady `Q=ṁΔh` becomes `Q=ṁΔh+dU_region/dt` (and analogous mass storage). Assign storage heat to physical metal regions through the derived profile/storage Jacobian, not an arbitrary global delta-U dump. Phase-zone changes, valve isolation and reversal need differentiable/conservative event treatment. Thus this route can reuse current enthalpy-integral component laws but is not merely adding a third root to cycle.ts and declaring dynamics conserved.

For an operating reference, a charge-balanced steady initializer must solve fluid/metal boundary conditions plus mass and the selected controller/valve law. Its M,U values are the initial stored inventory; subsequent accepted steps integrate them conservatively. Fixed geometry plus a generic reference operating point may define a **generic nominal charge**. A factory/nameplate mass is an independently sourced input/reference, not a proof that the generic coil geometry/distribution is correct.

The source register reports an N8-family SKU with 1.8 kg charge and an installer instruction to use actual nameplate charge plus any required extra amount. Exact owner suffix/serial nameplate and internal volumes remain unknown. A user may select 1.8 kg as a **generic charge scenario** only with that distinction and an admissible initialization. Do not invent a residual reservoir to force an OEM match, or label a tuned geometry match as validated performance. Oil-dissolved refrigerant and unspecified cavities explain part of possible hardware/model disagreement but cannot be silently fitted without evidence. [Charge source register and primary manufacturer links](refrigerant-charge-source-register.md).

## Integration and feasibility gates

First disposable pilot: two role-labelled regional reservoirs, evolving finite metals, compressor, fixed-opening HEM EEV and separate rigid-vessel leak test. This pilot does not implement physical reversal, multisplit spatial inventory, SH control, a separately connected accumulator or film/room integration. Its low reservoir has an explicit vapor-selective pickup, so it exercises separator bookkeeping but not an entire accumulator topology. The next prototype must retain fixed physical coil/line/accumulator identities and implement four-way connectivity before claiming heating/cooling/defrost coverage. Use transactional **implicit backward Euler** on conserved M,U plus algebraic flows/valve controls. Test line search, scaled residuals and numerical EOS Jacobians initially; exploit graph sparsity/local derivatives later only if measured cost warrants. A candidate fluid/metal/film step must solve together or iteratively converge so the exact same refrigerant heat crosses both boundaries. Lagged heat evaluated twice with inconsistent signs is not acceptable.

Use adaptive accepted substeps and error estimation by step doubling. Bound controller update intervals and valve slew rates by stated generic actuator assumptions, not by simulation-speed scaling. Positivity constraints and EOS-domain limits guide line search/event subdivision. Do not clip negative M, erase liquid, cap charge or rebase energy after accepting a bad step. Find phase/headspace/exhaustion/pressure-limit events and integrate to them; retain the last accepted state on failure. Numeric face-flow regularization near Δp=0 must recover physical laminar/valve behavior, preserve identical paired fluxes and be quantitatively insensitive at resolved operating flows.

Estimate stiffness from actual pilot states: `τ_turnover=M/Σ|ṁ|`, `τ_thermal≈M c_eff/UA` where a sensible derivative is defined, and pressure equilibration from compressibility and hydraulic conductance. Small vapor/header volumes may have timescales far below the existing 0.25 s explicit step. A fixed tiny explicit timestep across days of slow leaks is not a browser-feasible assumption. Multi-rate outer room/frost/controller integration may reuse converged inner fluid results, but may not drop elapsed physical time or omit stored-energy change.

For idealized line transport, retain physical volumes and use explicit zero-pressure-loss/adiabatic constraints or a reviewed conservative grouped-volume reduction. Do not introduce an undocumented artificial resistance merely to make local pressure solves easier. Physical transport instead uses the corresponding finite hydraulic/thermal links. Mode connectivity and near-zero flows require a dedicated well-posedness check in either route. Pilot both routes before claiming a transport toggle preserves the same charge model.

Observed local pinned-backend smoke check: three pure-R32 states (vapor at800kPa/5°C; liquid at2.5MPa/30°C; two-phase at800kPa/Q=.5), each 20 repeated public `(D,U)→T,P` double calls:

| State | Max T roundtrip error (K) | Max p error (Pa) | 2-call p50 / observed p95 (ms) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vapor | 2.50×10⁻⁷ | 0.00106 | 0.373 / 26.342 |
| Liquid | 5.68×10⁻¹⁴ | 8.99×10⁻⁸ | 0.379 / 0.625 |
| Two-phase | 0 | 8.15×10⁻¹⁰ | 0.414 / 2.874 |

The bundle exports `DmassUmass_INPUTS`, but this smoke check used public `PropsSI` only and does not validate unsafe low-level invalid trials. Costs include startup/cold-query variation on the shared machine. This is not an entire operating-domain flash grid, implicit-solver benchmark or browser throughput proof.

A complete Newton iteration over tens of cells can require many more calls than these two per cell; finite-difference global Jacobians can be prohibitively expensive. Measure actual call count, nonlinear iterations, rejected steps, p50/p95 wall time and memory for 1/3/6 rooms, normal operation, near-empty/near-full liquid states, reversal and defrost. Keep browser worker backpressure and actual accepted simulated-time accounting. Benchmark safe cached/EOS local-block Jacobians against any proposed generated table/native error-return wrapper, with independent thermodynamic derivative and energy consistency checks; never substitute an unvalidated fast property route.

## Required acceptance tests

1. **Closed charge:** no leak/service flow, arbitrary allowed weather/mode/Off events: total refrigerant mass constant; independent global stored-energy ledger includes ΣU_ref. Conservation is tested from accepted physical states and integrated boundary fluxes, not by constructing only `Qcond−Qevap=Pcomp`.
2. **Open charge:** prescribed local leaks/recovery/addition: mass loss/gain and transported h agree with independently integrated boundary values; sealed adiabatic single-phase blowdown and two-phase boiling cases match reference solutions; ambient-pressure/phase/exhaustion events reject or stop by the declared supported boundary.
3. **Positive inventory:** every cell M>0, V>0, liquid/vapor quantities nonnegative, sums consistent; accumulator headspace/pickup limits, dry compressor admission and no after-the-fact clipping. Test very low charge, condenser flooding and near-full liquid volumes.
4. **Control degrees:** nominal SH tracking only when actuator authority permits; SC is an output; undercharge valve saturation and loss of liquid supply are visible, overcharge storage/pressure response is geometry-dependent. Compare a steady mass-balance initializer with dynamic convergence at fixed boundaries. No exact SH+SC+charge overconstraint and no monotonic COP/linear penalty fixture.
5. **Geometry:** actual line volume affects inventory even with idealized pressure/heat transport; transport toggle preserves M,U and time; geometry edits/removals follow explicit recovery/initialization semantics. Inactive room volumes retain mass and passive thermodynamics. Port capacity constrains connections, not total charge or automatic thermal scaling.
6. **Thermal coupling:** opposite fluid/metal heat terms agree; current room H/w, fan heat, film latent plateau/drains remain consistent. Frost and defrost tests include the existing natural warm-owner trajectory, minimum-on/off and All Off during reversal/defrost without lost M/U/ice.

## Concrete reduced implementation candidate after the first pilot

The pressure-grouped finite-volume DAE is the preferred next **prototype**, ahead of an algebraic whole-coil phase-zone manifold. It preserves local physical cells while removing artificial pressure-loss coefficients from idealized transport. For K fluid cells in G connected pressure regions, use G pressure unknowns, K cell enthalpies and K−G internal tree-face flows; boundary compressor/EEV fluxes are constitutive. For each cell compute `M_j=ρ(p_g,h_j)V_j`, `U_j=M_j h_j−p_g V_j`, and solve **both** backward-Euler mass and energy equations using stored previous M/U. There are 2K equations and 2K unknowns. Additional metal temperatures/controllers have their own equations. This is an index-sensitive DAE reduction to test, not an assertion that arbitrary networks are well posed. Looped idealized hydraulic graphs require additional momentum constraints; a spanning tree cannot arbitrarily decide unknown parallel-branch circulation.

Cells at common pressure retain different h/T/quality. A condenser can accept hot vapor and discharge liquid; an evaporator can have wet inlet and dry outlet. The implicit internal flows supply each cell's actual accumulation, with upwind enthalpy for reverse flow. A closed-EEV/gas-connected branch is a dead-end tree: inferred transient gas migration changes its retained mass/energy but there is no prescribed steady throughflow. This is substantially more concrete than charging a shared perfectly mixed high reservoir while presenting invented coil endpoints.

Minimum production graph to develop next: three axial cells per physical coil; one cell per physical field line; one separate accumulator; only explicitly required junction cavities. Treat negligible-storage idealized headers as conservative mixing junctions rather than automatically add small stiff volumes. Refine only where spatial/temporal comparison shows material error. For one room this is nine fluid cells before non-negligible cavities; three rooms are nineteen. Shared pressure constraints reduce state complexity and reuse the existing safe PH property route, although nonlinear solves still have 2K variables and a dense finite-difference Jacobian will not scale well. Implement graph-local Jacobian blocks and continuation only after the pilot establishes the physical residuals. Physical pipe mode adds the independently bounded hydraulic relations; volume remains identical in both modes.

Four-way transitions retain the same physical cells and previous M/U. The parent controls compressor-off reversing dwell; bidirectional valve/accumulator restrictions permit redistribution. Subsequent generic low-lift transfer can create the new normal compression ordering without an inventory or pressure reset. Do not swap pL/pH labels onto old cell states or infer successful reversal from the role-labelled pilot. One-active/one-Off, full coupled reversal and natural frost/defrost remain separate gates.

An algebraic phase-profile alternative could retain fewer variables by expressing cell inventories as functions of shared p, endpoints and flow. It must derive the storage Jacobian and distribute `dM/dt,dU/dt` along the profile in the same heat-transfer equation. A profile solved from steady `ṁdh=UAΔT` while adding its changed U afterward is not that derivation. At present the finite-cell DAE gives a simpler auditable conservative closure; there is no measured justification to implement a more intricate moving-zone/storage Jacobian solely to save unproven CPU cost.

## Implemented charge foundation and current evidence

`src/engine/charge.ts` implements a pressure-grouped finite-volume DAE with `K=5N+4` physical fluid cells: three outdoor coil cells, gas line + three indoor coil cells + small line per room, and one accumulator. The outdoor, indoor/field and accumulator pressures belong to fixed physical regions. They remain attached to their volumes when heating/cooling connectivity changes. The negligible-storage indoor gas header adds one conservative mixing constraint; the resulting solve has `2K+1` unknowns/equations. Internal tree-face flows emerge from accumulation and upwind enthalpy; a shut EEV leaves a retained gas-connected branch.

The initializer distributes the requested total mass at uniform density and the explicitly selected fluid temperature. Its EOS pressure/enthalpy/internal energy are calculated rather than prescribed independently. The input is initial charge for a new scenario, not an operation that scales an existing state. Geometry is cloned/frozen, and a physical volume remains `πD²L/4` in hydraulically idealized field lines. Coil/cavity volumes are generic assumptions; the Daikin factory-charge reference does not validate those volumes. The foundation currently excludes field friction, line ambient heat/elevation, slip, oil and arbitrary high/near-critical operating regimes. The parent must explicitly gate the physical transport option for this formulation.

Core test geometry uses 6 mm internal coil tubing with 40 m outdoor and 10 m per indoor coil, 0.6 L accumulator, generic maximum EEV area `5e−7 m²`, accumulator inlet area `1e−4 m²`, coefficient .65, displacement `22e−6 m³/rev`, ηvol .8 and ηis .7. These are declared component assumptions, not catalogue internals or fitted factory-charge coefficients. Operational UA/compressor/controller parameters can change through `ChargeBoundary.operating` without reconstructing physical storage.

The actuator is a bounded integral servo: opening rate `.005*(SH−target)` per second, capped at ±.1/s and within [0,1]. It is not a tuned manufacturer controller. Off closes its EEV; reenabling prepositions a previously shut valve at .1/s when compressor operation is requested. Carryover protection takes precedence: while actual accumulator admission is held, each enabled EEV closes at .1/s, and a positive speed request cannot preposition it open. Actual admission recovery restores ordinary control. The close-to-prevent-feed concept is supported by the [Danfoss EKD316 manual, printed page6](https://assets.danfoss.com/documents/latest/238784/AG443454854521en-FR0101.pdf); this model's criterion/rate are generic assumptions, not Daikin sequencing. This policy is scoped to carryover protection and does not impose the same closure on every reversing or operator-Off state. Cooling uses the corresponding indoor evaporator gas-end bulk sensor; heating uses the common outdoor gas-end bulk sensor. The actual coil boundary stream and bulk sensor can differ during accumulation. SH/SC are outputs and controller goals; both are not imposed alongside total charge.

The accumulator is homogeneous-equilibrium bulk storage with a separate top vapor compressor pickup. A persistent generic protection latch closes admission at vapor volume fraction α=.02 and restores it at α=.04. Both thresholds are assumptions, not measured carryover values. Admission remains fixed during each nonlinear solve. A trial crossing is rejected and the interval shortened toward the physical event (α tolerance 1e−7); all pre-event M/U persist, and only the discrete admission state changes. Operator Off and valve reorientation do not clear the latch. Wet admitted pickup supplies saturated vapor; dry pickup uses actual h. Full-liquid pickup cannot supply compressor flow. Passive storage remains valid in every phase.

The separate accumulator inlet/reverse outlet always transports actual bulk HEM fluid. This is an explicit generic mixed/unseparated inlet assumption, distinct from the top dry pickup, not a claim about the actual Daikin inlet geometry. It avoids an unsupported vapor-source discontinuity at nearly zero headspace. Successful trial snapshots remain immutable and prepare/restore includes the admission latch.

`prepareStep(dt,boundary)` prepares the full requested `dt≤.25 s` without changing committed storage. Accepted internal backward-Euler substeps can be shortened after rejected Newton trials. `commit(candidate)` accepts only a candidate issued by that circuit at the current revision; candidates and snapshots are immutable, and `restore` accepts only that circuit's own snapshot. The parent prepares its thermal/moisture/film candidates before committing fluid storage. Failures preserve all inventories and accepted time.

Candidate heat is **integrated joules into fluid**, opposite metal/film heat. Compressor work and exported leak enthalpy use the same accepted face quadrature. A prescribed leak removes local mass and local h at the named physical cell, including while Off. Ambient-pressure ingress is rejected against `ChargeBoundary.ambientPressurePa` (default 101325 Pa for isolated fixtures). All faces use the same h for their paired outgoing/incoming balances.

Candidate coil ports expose upwind physical **boundary streams**, terminal bulk cell states, and separate signed gas/small face flows. Positive means into that coil. Since fluid accumulates, inlet/outlet flows and branch/compressor flows need not match instantaneously. `fieldPorts` separately exposes gas-header→gas-line flow and actual retained gas/small line states; field-line→coil flow is a different face. Headers mix only entering streams and are null if no incoming stream exists. At zero flow terminal states are static inventories, not circulating-temperature predictions. Downstream low-lift fluid can legitimately be wet even when pickup was dry. The `lowLiftTransfer` flag and diagnostics aggregate every accepted internal substep; the parent must make COP unavailable for such an interval.

The restrictors use a maximum homogeneous-equilibrium isentropic mass flux, with downstream kinetic energy dissipated in the receiving control volume. A finite equivalent circular passage adds the low-flow resistance `R=8πμL/(ρA²)` in mass-flow units, derived from the single-phase laminar [Poiseuille law](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-7-viscosity-and-turbulence). The default equivalent lengths are .005 m for the EEV and .1 m for the accumulator inlet; these are declared generic dimensions. With `m_HEM=C_d A G_max`, available pressure drop `Δp_t=p_up−p_throat`, and `K=Δp_t/m_HEM²`, the positive root satisfies `Δp_t=R m+K m²`. At tiny pressure drop it approaches `m=Δp/R`; once choked the correction uses the throat drop and preserves the backpressure plateau. This is an equivalent viscous/inertial series reduction, not an exact coupled viscous flashing nozzle: the upstream state/throat is not recalculated after the viscous loss. Wet viscosity is an arithmetic mass-quality mixture surrogate, unvalidated as a two-phase laminar law. Actual field lines receive no artificial resistance.

The nonlinear solver evaluates real PH residuals. Pressure intervals start at relative `1e−9`; the analytic route retains this small interval for face-flux derivatives in all phases. Only the finite-difference comparison route uses a 100-times interval for regions containing a single-phase cell. Enthalpy intervals stay within the current local bubble/dew regime where representable. Tenfold pressure-interval variations are checked in the startup test. Actual PH derivative audit at 8.0964124 bar showed subcooled-liquid centered density slopes at .001 Pa with +66.6-fold error and .01 Pa with the wrong sign, despite correct thermodynamic states; correlated stored mass/energy uncertainty was about 1e−10 kg/20µJ in an outdoor cell. Increasing the whole pressure perturbation would distort the finite restrictor's near-zero slope. The production Jacobian instead subtracts finite-difference **storage and local coil-temperature** contributions and adds exact EOS derivatives, while retaining finite differences of face fluxes. Single-phase density/temperature partials use the documented safe public derivative interface; pure-fluid wet storage uses the differentiated equilibrium lever rule with valid Q=0/1 saturation derivatives. Wet pure-fluid temperature satisfies `T_h=0`, `T_p=dT_sat/dp`. For each energy row the local heat correction is `dt*UA*(T'_exact−T'_FD)`, because its residual includes `+dt*UA*T`; the aggregate energy row receives the same summed correction. General partial derivatives are not requested inside coexistence. At exact bubble/dew, each pressure/enthalpy column selects the derivative for its actual one-sided trial branch without moving the physical state. [CoolProp derivative definitions and phase limits](https://coolprop.org/coolprop/HighLevelAPI.html#partial-derivatives).

For `M=Vρ` and `U=V(ρh−p)`, the storage slopes are `M_p=Vρ_p`, `M_h=Vρ_h`, `U_p=V(hρ_p−1)`, `U_h=V(ρ+hρ_h)`. In wet pure fluid, `v=(1−x)/ρ_l+x/ρ_v` and `x=(h−h_l)/(h_v−h_l)` provide both slopes using saturation-curve derivatives. No EOS value is approximated, cached by rounded input, or adjusted to meet conservation.

Newton uses squared scaled residual as its line-search merit, with separate physical balance acceptance:

- Each original cell mass: `1e−10 + 1e−10*M_previous` kg.
- Each original cell energy: `1e−5 + 1e−9*abs(U_previous) + max(abs(h_previous),abs(h_trial))*M_tolerance` J. The linked term follows `U=Mh−pV`: accepted native mass uncertainty propagates into stored energy. Aggregate/cumulative energy limits remain independent.
- Zero-storage header integrated mass: `1e−10` kg.
- Independent aggregate step mass: `5e−11` kg; aggregate step energy: `1e−5` J.
- Cumulative core ledger guard: `abs(R_M)≤1e−7 kg`, `abs(R_U)≤.01 J`.

The Newton system replaces the accumulator mass/energy rows with aggregate physical balances. This is equation preconditioning: summed original balances differ by the zero-storage header continuity row (and its enthalpy multiple for energy), so the same zero set remains. The finite-difference Jacobian differentiates the transformed residual. Acceptance independently verifies **all original cell balances**, including the replaced accumulator rows, plus the header, aggregate step and cumulative ledger constraints. No state or residual is repaired. Near-equal-pressure passive draining exposed a native PH density residual of 6.2e−11 kg; the bounded local 1e−10 kg floor acknowledges that precision while the separately enforced aggregate constraint protects total charge. Exact passive equilibrium is evaluated before any active startup guess, which prevents a requested-but-protected compressor from perturbing an already balanced circuit.

These are numerical acceptance budgets, never M/U repairs or ledger rebases. Earlier uniform scaled tolerance `1e−11` demanded sub-microjoule balances and exhausted adaptation at a real EOS/derivative precision floor, despite closed cumulative ledgers. Failure messages now identify the worst physical equation, units and tolerance. Historical attempts to tighten only local mass tolerances did not solve the PH precision floor and are superseded by aggregate equation conditioning. The cumulative .1 mg/.01 J limits remain explicit and must be checked independently.
