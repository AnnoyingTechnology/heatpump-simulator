# Primary-source register

Research snapshot: 2026-10-10. This register records sources opened during the primary-source collection pass and the sections useful to the simulation work. Manufacturer specifications and library coverage are version-specific; verify again before turning them into defaults. `Observed` below means the source itself was opened, not that hardware was inspected.

## Cycle, multi-split construction, and frost

### Daikin 3MXM-N installer reference guide, 4P600463-1, 2019-10

[Official Daikin PDF](https://www.daikin.eu/content/dam/document-library/Installer-reference-guide/ac/split/3MXM-N%2C%203AMXM-M%2C%203AMXF-A%2C%203MXF-A%2C%204MXM-N%2C%205MXM-N_4PEN600463-1_2019_10_Installer%20reference%20guide_English.pdf). This opened guide cover explicitly names outdoor variant **3MXM52N2V1B**. It is a useful exact-family circuit/installation source and matches the full variant used in Daikin's European performance table, but it is a 2019 N2 guide, not proof that the owner's later-marketed 3MXM52N8 has identical revisions or controls.

Daikin's official [3MXM-N product documentation page](https://www.daikin.eu/en_us/products/product.html/3mxm-n.html) was opened and currently lists the 2019 4PEN600463-1 guide. An official search-index record also exposed a candidate 2020 4P600463-1C guide whose title includes 3MXM-N8, but its English URL on `my.daikin.eu` returns HTTP 404 when opened; equivalent likely paths on `daikin.eu`, `daikin.co.uk`, `daikin.fr`, and `daikin-ce.com` also returned 404 to Firefox-UA requests. Do not treat the indexed snippet as read manual content. Thus an opened official N8 schematic/service drawing was not recovered in this pass; use N2 diagram only as qualified family context and leave N8-specific internal topology/revision unknown.

- §5.2.3, printed p.10 / PDF lines 989–1029: minimum required room area 4.7 m² for 3MXM52 family; each branch 3–25 m; total length up to 50 m; height differences depend on which unit is higher (outdoor higher: outdoor–indoor ≤15 m and indoor–indoor ≤7.5 m; outdoor lower than at least one indoor: outdoor–indoor ≤7.5 m and indoor–indoor ≤15 m).
- §5.3.1, printed p.11 / PDF lines 1042–1046: for 3MXM52 group the field piping interfaces are three Ø6.4 mm liquid connections, one Ø9.5 mm gas and two Ø12.7 mm gas connections.
- §6.4.7–6.4.8, printed pp.16–17 / PDF lines 1598–1625: service connection is Schrader type and requires a charge hose with valve-depressor pin; the connection figure labels the service port at the gas stop valve. In the model-specific §12.2 circuit schematic (printed p.29), the 3MXM40/52 circuit has one common gas stop valve `p` and one liquid stop valve `q` on the outdoor unit, with three A/B/C branches. Thus this guide supports one common gas-side service connection for the illustrated 3MXM52N2V1B topology, not one independent service port per indoor branch. Confirm the exact N8 hardware/revision from its own manual/nameplate before making this an N8 physical assertion.
- §6.6.4–6.6.5, printed p.19 / PDF lines 1834–1857: no extra refrigerant for total one-way liquid piping length ≤30 m; above 30 m, additional mass is `(total one-way liquid length − 30 m) × 0.020 kg/m`, rounded to 0.1 kg. Maximum allowable charge for 3MXM52 group is 2.2 kg; complete recharge uses factory charge from unit nameplate plus calculated additional mass.
- §12.2, printed p.29 / PDF lines 3360–3413: model-specific schematic is explicitly grouped “3MXM40, 3MXM52, 3AMXM52, 3MXF52, 3AMXF52”; it identifies outdoor heat exchanger, fan, capillary tubes, distributor, refnet header, compressor, accumulator, four-way valve, the common gas/liquid stop valves, and three A/B/C electronic expansion valves. The field liquid branches connect on the EEV side of the diagram; heating/cooling flow arrows are both shown. This is sufficient to avoid a false indoor-only expansion-valve diagram, but not a controller-state or complete refrigerant-phase table.

Use the guide’s labels carefully: “field piping – liquid” and “field piping – gas” label line connections, while heating/cooling flow arrows are separately marked. They do not prove a fixed thermodynamic phase in either line in both modes. The 2019 N2 circuit diagram shows the EEVs on the outdoor branch liquid-side circuit, plus a four-way valve; do not replace this with an assumed universal three-way selector.

### Daikin Split catalogue 2019–2020

[Official Daikin-hosted catalogue PDF](https://my.daikin.eu/content/dam/dace-hr/literature/Katalog_Split_2019-2020.pdf), published by Daikin Central Europe; the opened edition is Croatian-language, but model codes, numeric tables, and units are legible.

- Printed p.43 / PDF page 42, lines 1973–2032: catalogue says up to five indoor units can be independently controlled and need not be installed at once; connected-unit matrix lists 3MXM52N and FTXA-A sizes. Outdoor dimensions row groups 3MXM52N with the 734 × 958 × 340 mm enclosure; net-weight row gives 41 kg for 3MXM52N; it lists R32, nominal factory charge 1.80 kg, liquid/gas outside diameters 6.35/9.5 mm, 25 m max per indoor branch, charge threshold >30 m, and 0.02 kg/m beyond. These are 2019–20 published family data, not a measurement of the owner’s serial-numbered equipment.
- Printed pp.54–55 / PDF pages 53–54, lines 2651–2800: 3MXM52N combination tables for cooling and heating, model identifier 3MXM52N2V1B. The exact nominal 2.5 + 2.5 + 3.5 combination is present in both tables. Heating row: nominal indoor allocations 2.00 + 2.00 + 2.80 kW, 6.80 kW total, 1.35 kW input and COP 5.05. Cooling row: nominal allocations 1.53 + 1.53 + 2.14 kW, 5.20 kW total, 1.04 kW input and EER 5.02. These are catalogue test ratings; they are not hourly outputs or guaranteed room-level allocations under arbitrary conditions. The opened table does not state the test-point conditions alongside each row, so do not present them as an operating map.
- Printed p.43 matrix supports FTXA type/size compatibility with the 3MXM52N family. The catalogue’s exact performance combination row is identified by nominal indoor classes rather than a complete set of FTXA color/market suffixes.

### Reversing and defrost context

- [U.S. DOE/BTO project presentation, “Quantifying and Reducing Defrost Energy Use”](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/bto-peer-2024-32275-quantifying-and-reducing-defrost-energy-use-woods.pdf), opened from search result: describes frost removal by reversing refrigerant cycle (temporary cooling-mode operation); the figure labels indoor and outdoor coils and reverse-cycle defrost. This is an official project presentation, not a detailed control algorithm.
- [ASHRAE Cold Climate Conference paper](https://www.ashrae.org/File%20Library/Conferences/Specialty%20Conferences/Cold%20Climate%202023%20-%20Papers/ASHRAE-D-CCC23-12.pdf), opened from search result: describes temporarily reversing cycle to provide heat to outdoor coil and reports ductless-unit defrost energy analysis. The copy carries an ASHRAE personal-use notice; cite and summarize without reproducing it.
- The Daikin technical schematic independently supports a four-way valve in the 3MXM40/52 outdoor circuit. The DOE source supports reverse-cycle defrost as a documented heat-pump mechanism. Neither source gives Daikin’s frost sensor thresholds, initiation algorithm, termination logic, timing, or exact indoor-fan policy for this model; keep those control details unknown unless a model-specific manual is found.

## Refrigerant properties and browser feasibility

### CoolProp official documentation and source project

- [CoolProp fluid list and pure/pseudo-pure property overview](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/PurePseudoPure.html), opened. It lists distinct pure-fluid and pseudo-pure formulations and linked property references. The opened list includes R22, R32, R134a, R1234yf, R290 via `n-Propane`, plus pseudo-pure R407C and R410A.
- [CoolProp mixture documentation](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/Mixtures.html), opened; section “Predefined mixtures” gives R407C = R32 + R125 + R134a with mole fractions 0.381109 + 0.179559 + 0.439332, and R410A = R32 + R125 with mole fractions 0.697615 + 0.302385. These are mole fractions, not mass fractions. Separate mass composition commonly reported for R407C is 23/25/52 and R410A 50/50; do not substitute those numbers into CoolProp’s mole-fraction fields. The page lists many mixtures with missing pure fluids or missing binary interaction parameters: a fluid-name entry alone is not enough to enable a credible blend model.
- [CoolProp R407C property reference](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/R407C.html), opened; EOS reference is E.W. Lemmon (2003), “Pseudo-Pure Fluid Equations of State for the Refrigerant Blends R-410A, R-404A, R-507A, and R-407C,” DOI [10.1023/A:1025048800563](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1025048800563). The page identifies R407C as pseudo-pure and reports ASHRAE class A1.
- [CoolProp R410A property reference](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/R410A.html), opened; same 2003 pseudo-pure EOS reference. Page reports R410A as A1.
- [CoolProp R32 property reference](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/R32.html), opened; its generated page reports safety class **A2**, which conflicts with the current ASHRAE table below. Treat the CoolProp field as discrepant library metadata, not the authoritative safety classification.
- [CoolProp R22 property reference](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/R22.html), opened; reports A1, gives its EOS reference (Kamei, Beyerlein & Jacobsen, 1995), and reports “Flash consistency (HEOS): 10 inconsistent” on the current generated page. This warns against blindly assuming every legacy-fluid calculation is numerically robust at all state points; test the selected cycle domain.
- [CoolProp R134a property reference](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/R134a.html), opened; gives the Tillner-Roth & Baehr (1994) standard formulation and A1 class.
- [CoolProp R1234yf property reference](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/R1234yf.html), opened; page’s reference section links the current pure-fluid EOS/transport models, includes REFPROP comparison plots and consistency plots. Source shows a usable pure-fluid property implementation, not that the fluid is a historically common residential split-system charge.
- [CoolProp `n-Propane` property page](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/n-Propane.html) is the target for R290 (propane), because `R290.html` is not a CoolProp page. The official list and docs use `n-Propane`; any UI label R290 should map deliberately to this pure-fluid identity.
- [CoolProp JavaScript wrapper documentation](https://github.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/blob/master/Web/coolprop/wrappers/Javascript/index.rst), opened. It documents precompiled `coolprop.js` and `coolprop.wasm`, ES module loading, browser use, and local-server MIME configuration (`application/wasm`). It also documents building the wrapper with Emscripten. This is primary project documentation and establishes a possible fully local/static deployment route. GitHub Pages can serve static assets, but the actual chosen artifact size, license notices, initialization behavior, and browser performance still need project validation.
- [CoolProp JavaScript example](https://github.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/blob/master/Web/coolprop/wrappers/Javascript/index.html), opened; demonstrates asynchronous module initialization and `PropsSI` calls from browser code. The example itself imports local JS/WASM files; it does not require a runtime API.
- [CoolProp 8.0.0 JavaScript release directory](https://sourceforge.net/projects/coolprop/files/CoolProp/8.0.0/Javascript/), opened; lists `coolprop.js` at 167.1 kB and `coolprop.wasm` at 9.2 MB, plus the sample HTML. The official [CoolProp 8.0.0 release notes](https://github.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/releases) identify v8 as a major release and warn that the WASM/JavaScript wrapper changed mixture-composition and phase-envelope data to native JS arrays, removing `VectorDouble` and `VectorString`. Pin a release and test the exact pair/API instead of copying an older example blindly. The [CoolProp MIT license](https://github.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/blob/master/LICENSE) is also opened; redistribution should retain its copyright and permission notice, and check bundled dependency licenses for the selected artifact.
- [CoolProp humid-air properties](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/HumidAir.html), opened from official CoolProp search results; section “Sample HAPropsSI Code” and ASHRAE-RP1485 basis. Suitable as a reference for moist-air calculations, but must verify that selected build exposes required humid-air methods in browser wrapper.
- [CoolProp high-level interface](https://coolprop.org/coolprop/HighLevelAPI.html), opened; names `PropsSI`, phase/saturation workflows and mixture interfaces. Documented support still needs runtime coverage tests in the exact browser artifact.
- [CoolProp advanced transport properties](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/more_reading.html), opened. It cautions that only a limited fluid selection has direct viscosity/conductivity correlations and extended corresponding states may estimate transport properties for others. Cycle enthalpy/pressure calculations and realistic refrigerant-side UA/pressure-drop estimates therefore have different confidence levels.

### R407C glide primary evidence

- [NISTIR 5873](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir5873.pdf), opened; table “Selected Properties of Tested Refrigerants at Atmospheric Pressure”: R22 has 0 °C bubble/dew glide; R407C (R32/R125/R134a 23/25/52 mass percent) has −43.8/−36.7 °C bubble/dew values and 7.1 °C glide in that listed atmospheric-pressure comparison. The report says its property values were calculated from REFPROP subroutines; use it as a historical primary technical report, not as an all-conditions property map.
- [NIST conference paper, “Potential Coefficient of Performance Improvements due to Glide Matching With R-407C”](https://www.nist.gov/publications/potential-coefficient-performance-improvements-due-glide-matching-r-407c), opened; assesses COP impact of matching temperature glide to heat-exchanger temperature profile. Its consequence for model scope: zeotropic blends require distinct bubble/dew states and finite-temperature-profile treatment if heat exchangers are to reflect glide; assigning a single saturation temperature discards a material physical feature.
- [CoolProp bibliography entry for Lemmon et al.](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1025048800563), above, is the EOS source underpinning the current R407C and R410A pseudo-pure implementation. R410A’s near-azeotropic behavior is distinct from R407C’s larger glide; use the property backend’s dew/bubble behavior rather than a generic “blend glide” constant.

### Scope by refrigerant family

The current official CoolProp docs provide an auditable property route for the specific historical and requested examples: R22; R407C and R410A as pseudo-pure blends; R32, R134a and R1234yf as pure fluids; R290 as `n-Propane`. The backend’s listed presence is necessary, not sufficient: the simulator still needs an explicit selected range, validity/consistency review, cycle-state coverage, blend bubble/dew handling, and property calls tested in the browser build.

For the “past ~30 years” interpretation, retain these as distinct *historical/common refrigerant candidates* rather than imply universal prevalence in residential air-source split heat pumps: R22 is a legacy HCFC; R407C/R410A became common HFC alternatives; R32 is a later lower-GWP HFC; R290 is a hydrocarbon candidate with a different flammability/safety envelope; R134a is included as a long-established HFC and useful benchmark but was more common in other refrigeration/heat-pump applications than in small residential split AC; R1234yf is chiefly known from mobile A/C, and is included because owner explicitly requested it. The equipment/library sources here substantiate property support and selected device compatibility, not market-share time series or regulatory status. Do not turn this qualitative historical framing into market statistics.

## Standards and performance terms

- [ASHRAE Handbook—Refrigeration 2026, Chapter 1, “Halocarbon and Hydrocarbon Refrigeration Systems”](https://handbook.ashrae.org/Handbooks/R26/IP/r26_ch01/r26_ch01_ip.aspx), opened, Table 1 “ASHRAE Number and Safety Classification of Refrigerants”: lists R32 as A2L, R1234yf as A2L, R290 (propane) as A3, R134a as A1 and R410A as A1. Prefer this current authoritative classification table to the inconsistent CoolProp R32 metadata. It is not a complete hazard analysis or installation-code approval.
- [ASHRAE refrigerant designations overview](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/standards-and-guidelines/ashrae-refrigerant-designations), opened; explains that its public table is based on ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 34-2022 and may not be complete/current. Use the Handbook 2026 table for the named examples and verify applicable local safety requirements separately.

- [EU Commission Regulation 206/2012, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A32012R0206), opened; Annex I defines rated EER as declared cooling capacity divided by rated cooling power input, and rated COP similarly for heating, at standard rating conditions. It defines those conditions from indoor/outdoor dry-bulb and corresponding wet-bulb values (Annex II, Table 2). Use only to explain that manufacturer COP/EER ratings are operating-point results with defined test conditions, not universal device constants. Its definitions/rating framework are historical/currently archived law; check current applicable standards before making legal claims.
- [Eurovent Air Conditioners programme overview](https://www.eurovent-certification.com/en/cms/news/programme-focus-air-conditioners), opened; identifies EN 14511, EN 14825 and EN 12102 used in its current certification programme and notes multi-split certification options. It is a certification-body source, not the normative standards text.
