## Accumulator capacity and closure diagnosis

The accumulator already consumes saturated vapor while a finite equilibrium liquid pool remains, using the identical outgoing `m*h` in its M/U ledger and the compressor inlet. Incoming donor enthalpy is retained and flashes through the real-EOS reconstruction. Fully liquid inventory cannot supply vapor. Thus an absent compressor-side separator is not the diagnosed defect.

At cooling 126.25 s, saturation densities are 957.289/48.585 kg/m³. Of the declared 600 mL, liquid occupies 591.338 mL; remaining vapor mass is only 0.420826 g and gas volume fraction 0.014436. The declared 0.02 protection threshold corresponds to 0.563469 kg at that pressure, and full saturated-liquid capacity to 0.574373 kg. Final actual subcooled density 955.972 kg/m³ makes 0.573583 kg occupy the full 600 mL. During staged heating at 500 s, actual liquid density is 1062.666 kg/m³ and 0.637600 kg likewise occupies the full vessel. These are real capacity limits **within the declared instantaneous-equilibrium closure**; the 600 mL geometry and threshold are generic assumptions, not verified owner hardware.

Potential structural gaps remain distinct: instantaneous phase thermal equilibrium, adiabatic shell, unspecified physical inlet/pickup geometry and reverse-inlet donor phase, and homogeneous coil-end transport without gravity/slip. A phase-selective vessel outlet needs actual declared geometry and exhaustion events; naming a coil port “gas” does not justify separation. No capacity or charge changes are selected to make traces succeed.

A proposed nonequilibrium extension needs explicit common-pressure volume closure, finite gas availability, interphase transfer, moving phase-volume pressure work, paired wall heat and phase-exhaustion handling; it remains subject to independent review and EOS admissibility qualification. This source supports investigating the mechanism, not claiming it explains or cures these failures.
