## Charge-design acceptance gates

The existing solver has circulation-flow mass residuals and air/metal/water energy ledgers, but **no stored refrigerant mass or internal-energy state**. Their excellent current closure does not establish refrigerant inventory conservation. A charge/leak extension must distinguish those ledgers and enlarge the system boundary honestly.

1. Specify real component/line internal volumes, EOS density, phase-zone allocation and a named mass-quality/void-fraction closure. The current pure R32 `Qmass=Q` convention is valid only for that pure fluid. Do not transfer it to future mixtures. No manufacturer-exact internal volumes have been established.
2. Replace an appropriate control constraint or introduce a genuine inventory degree of freedom. Prescribed suction superheat and condenser subcooling plus the existing pressure/circulation closure cannot simply be augmented with arbitrary total charge while leaving every degree fixed. A reservoir inventory variable is legitimate only within its geometric capacity and phase/energy closure.
3. Track inactive branch holdup, mode reversal and redistribution. If a first supported model deliberately excludes these regimes, visibly gate them and preserve accepted state. A closed branch's refrigerant cannot silently disappear from total charge.
4. For a slow leak, remove actual mass and exported local-port enthalpy. Stored refrigerant energy uses internal energy (`u=h−p/ρ`), not simply `m h` for a rigid control volume. Re-solving a quasi-steady cycle changes stored internal energy and requires an explicit redistribution/configuration boundary term or physically closed transient coupling. Reporting only a separately integrated leak-enthalpy counter is not global energy conservation.
5. A user-imposed kg/time loss can be a useful explicitly imposed scenario, but is not a physical-orifice prediction. A pressure/phase-dependent orifice closure needs its location and discharge assumptions and guarded flashing/choking regimes. Reject negative inventory, wet compressor inlet, excess reservoir fill, pressure/discharge-envelope violations and unsupported convergence; do not impose a charge-to-capacity percentage multiplier.
6. Source manufacturer's baseline charge separately from generic inventory calibration. A generic residual volume or reference calibration must be named and bounded. It must not silently fit the real owner's nameplate and then imply validated charge sensitivity. Line-volume inventory remains nonzero even when line transport losses are idealized; these two assumptions require separate configuration paths.

The objection to independently redrawing a steady charge distribution with unaccounted energy is correct. It does **not** rule out every conservative quasi-steady DAE or grouped pressure-region reduction. Algebraic phase/inventory constraints may be coupled to integrated conserved M,U states and their derivatives, with pressure equilibration treated as unresolved instead of silently discarded. Such a reduction still needs specified constitutive equations and a nonsingular constrained solve; a global bookkeeping correction does not create physical closure.

The proposed default graph has about 44 fluid M,U unknowns for three rooms, before actuator and thermal states. The pilot should compare a few physically justified grouped-volume/DAE states with finite axial cells at the same boundary conditions, charge, controller settings and accepted simulated time. Keep the leaner conservative approach if it meets the required spatial observables and event behavior; retain additional cells only when refinement evidence justifies them. Do not treat full axial resolution as already necessary for owner-visible charge effects.

### CRG-02 — specify the EEV constitutive law before the one-room loop (high, unresolved)

A bounded opening and PI superheat controller are actuator/control definitions, not a flow law. The design correctly says a generic incompressible square-root law is inadequate across large pressure-drop flashing/choking, but the first draft does not yet select the proposed EEV law. No stable nominal/undercharge/overcharge operating result can be accepted without it.

Action: select and document a bounded liquid-upstream HEM nozzle/restrictor approximation or another primary-supported closure, including upstream phase/subcooling assumptions, flow area/opening, discharge coefficient, direction, critical mass-flux/choking criterion and recovered downstream enthalpy. Preserve upstream stagnation enthalpy in face energy transport while declaring unresolved jet kinetic energy/recovery. If only single-phase liquid entrance is supported initially, reject a vapor-fed or unsupported flashing entrance explicitly rather than making a charge penalty. Check reverse/near-zero Δp, liquid/two-phase entrances, opening limits and pressure-ratio limits with the actual EOS. A mechanical leak orifice is separately optional; the first imposed g/day leak does not require pretending to predict a pinhole.

### CRG-03 — accumulator is a phase-selective constitutive exception (medium, unresolved details)

The accumulator's gravity-separated vapor pickup deliberately differs from homogeneous bulk-stream transport. Its proposed outlet h_v and conserved whole-cavity M,U can be consistent, but “no-slip HEM throughout” must identify this phase-selective exception. Stored vapor mass is not an unlimited supply; equilibrium evaporation can replenish it only with corresponding internal-energy change. An arbitrary cap `ṁ≤M_v/Δt` would make the component's physical behavior depend on numerical timestep.

Action: define vapor headspace/pickup geometry, maximum pool fill and compressor-stop/carryover event surfaces. Integrate to those events or solve consistent complementarity constraints, with EOS phase equilibrium and no negative phase mass. State whether gravity only supplies the separation closure while gravitational storage energy is neglected, or include the latter consistently. Test saturated mixture evaporation into the pickup, approaching full-liquid fill, vapor-only operation and near-empty stopping. Declared adiabatic heat exchange is an acceptable first-pilot boundary.

### CRG-04 — initialize connected geometry explicitly; retain transactional EOS safety (medium, actionable)

Total M and an initial refrigerant temperature can initialize a connected equilibrium network once valve connectivity and any separated/isolated pocket assignment are defined. In a two-phase state, temperature fixes saturation pressure but total density/volume fixes quality; a guessed uniform liquid fraction is unnecessary and can be wrong. The first draft's unknown initial valve topology must be specified before claiming a repeatable initial charge. Separately initialized metal/air states are legitimate declared stored energy, not operating equilibrium evidence.

Action: first use an explicitly connected, uniform-temperature equilibrium fluid initialization with no active compressor; determine M,U/phase from actual finite volumes and EOS, including accumulator geometry. Reject overfilled/inadmissible states; do not silently add a hidden volume to accommodate 1.8 kg. Choose a separate documented operating initializer later. Safe public D,U flashes must preflight computed P/T, never their echoed input D/U. Any reusable AbstractState extension requires guarded invalid-trial/recovery checks; private ABI exception patches remain excluded.
