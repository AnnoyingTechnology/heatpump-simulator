### EN-01 — Medium: fixed sensible-zone quadrature under-resolves accepted near-pinch states

Affected initial source: `src/engine/cycle.ts:7–25` (initial three-point enthalpy quadrature).

An independent dense real-EOS enthalpy Simpson integral at R32 saturation 40°C, refrigerant inlet 100°C and outlet 37°C recovered errors of −1.2433% for metal 36.9°C and −4.9500% for metal 36.99°C. Both positive terminal approaches were accepted by the original routine. Required conductance diverges logarithmically near the sensible-zone endpoint; a fixed three-node rule without refinement can distort the flow and pressure closure even though the latent-zone test passes.

Recommended fix: resolve the endpoint behavior with transformed/adaptive quadrature and add a nonlatent near-pinch accuracy comparison; a phase-only latent identity is insufficient.

Outcome: **resolved in revised core**. Sensible zones now integrate real `cp(P,T)` in `z=log|T−Tmetal|` with ≤0.5 subdivisions. Independent 4000-interval enthalpy Simpson comparisons across metal 20/30/35/36.9/36.99°C agree within approximately 0.000003%; the independent reference's 2000→4000 refinement changes by at most approximately 0.000035%. Latent contribution remains analytic. This bounded check does not establish whole-envelope quadrature accuracy.

## Added field-line and cleanliness source review

`transport.ts` now implements finite smooth-copper Darcy/HEOS homogeneous-equilibrium transport with friction, gravity, acceleration momentum and distributed heat exchange, coupled into each branch. The earlier zero-line-only statement above describes the initial source, not current coverage. Current source bounds friction to 20% of local pressure, single-phase Mach to 0.3, homogeneous two-phase momentum sensitivity to 0.3, and omitted kinetic energy to 0.5% of latent enthalpy. These are declared numerical/model coverage guards rather than experimental validation. The steady-flow first law includes kinetic as well as static enthalpy; its omission must remain a bounded approximation, not an exact physical closure claim. [NASA steady-flow energy derivation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/conservation-of-energy/).

Two-phase homogeneous Darcy transport needs practical validation before interpreting installation pressure losses as accurate. NISTIR6333 independently compared pressure-loss correlations against refrigerant data including R32; its examined homogeneous correlation underpredicted losses by 31.4% on average. That result concerns a different correlation and dataset and is **not** an error estimate for this implementation. It demonstrates why a real EOS and tight conservation residual cannot substitute for validating the chosen two-phase correlation. Retain the current slip/oil/fittings exclusions and label computed losses as generic HEM estimates. [NISTIR6333, homogeneous comparison pp.9–10](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir6333.pdf).

## Revised idealized cooling component/control closure

Total flow starts from dry target-suction density × displacement × speed × volumetric efficiency. Each branch independently computes its **actual** finite-UA outlet enthalpy against its retained metal; branch outlets are mass-weighted before the shared superheat control. The pressure solve uses mixed-return superheat and actual outdoor-condenser closure, recomputes compressor work from actual mixed suction, and independently checks actual displacement-flow residual and dry suction before acceptance. Thus each branch is no longer forced to reach the same superheat; wet branch returns can physically mix with hotter vapor into a dry common return. This is a declared generic control allocation, not a manufacturer EEV map.

Permutation changed pressures by equivalent saturation-temperature differences <3×10⁻⁹ K and branch enthalpies <0.00018 J/kg; an inactive branch had zero flow and heat. Nonlimiting branches recover their specified UA through the independent inverse integral; numerically equilibrated endpoints use the documented limiting heat-error bound.
